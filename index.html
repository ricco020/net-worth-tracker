Net worth tracker

A net worth calculator gives you one number. What you need after the second month is why it moved: did you pay down debt, did you save, or did the market or the house estimate do the work? List your accounts and debts once, save a snapshot each month (only the balances change), and this page breaks the change since last month down line by line, with liquid net worth, the monthly average and how many months to your goal at that pace.

How it is calculated

Net worth = everything you own minus everything you owe, on the day of the snapshot. Use what each thing would sell for today: the account balance, not what you paid in, a realistic sale price for the home, not the purchase price, a used-car valuation for the car. Pending pensions you cannot cash out and the resale value of furniture and clothes are usually left out.

Liquid net worth here = cash plus taxable investments, minus the debts that are not secured by the home or a vehicle (cards, personal and student loans, money owed to family). Retirement accounts and the home are left out because turning them into cash costs tax, penalties or a move. Definitions vary from one site to another: this is the one used on this page.

What moved it: each line is matched with the same name and type in the previous snapshot (a new line counts from 0, a removed line goes to 0). An asset that grew adds to net worth, a debt that shrank adds to net worth. The lines always add up exactly to the change in net worth. The page cannot tell a deposit from a market gain inside the same account: if you want that split, keep contributions as a separate line.

Months to goal = (goal minus latest net worth) divided by the average monthly change between your first and latest snapshot. It is a straight line, not a forecast: one big market month in either direction moves it a lot, so read it after 6 snapshots or more.
